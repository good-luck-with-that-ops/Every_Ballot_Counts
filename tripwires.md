## Since Monday night
- **A federal judge paused the federal voter-list pipeline itself, not just the ballot-hold letters** (D, League of Women Voters v. DHS, D.D.C., Judge Chutkan, order ECF 30 and opinion ECF 31, Nov 2). In the court's own words, DHS and DOJ "have collected millions of voting records—including those of U.S. citizens," and "the government is now transmitting selected portions to states and localities." The order bars "further disclosing, transmitting, or releasing" lists of alleged noncitizen or registered voters to any state or local election official; it still allows SAVE checks and prosecutions, and DOJ must name "a senior official" responsible for compliance within 24 hours. Likely Privacy Act violation: no published notice (SORN), and DOJ is "unlikely" to fit the law-enforcement exception. (The "national database" and "47 states" descriptions are the plaintiffs' allegations, which the court recounts.)
- **The order protecting election officials from federal threats survived the night by one step** (D, Ninth Circuit orders, Nov 2). A 2-1 Ninth Circuit panel paused Judge Thompson's order late Monday, calling DOJ's letters and warnings "core Executive functions"; hours later the full court vacated that pause and took the question itself. Thompson's order stands, now through 5 p.m. Nov 13.
- **DOJ's own lawyer agreed it is "fair" to say the California "noncitizen" list has a high error rate** (R, Law Dork, Bloomberg Law, Nov 2 hearing).
- **DOJ poll monitors must be "administrative or clerical personnel and not law enforcement officials"** (D, NAACP v. Blanche, D.D.C., Judge Walton, Nov 2): unarmed, without "the power to effectuate arrests," no agency insignia, and they may "not talk with any voter inside the polling place." Walton denied the NAACP's request (finding plain-clothes, non-law-enforcement monitors would not intimidate a reasonable voter) but said that view "would differ" if DOJ sent officers with arrest authority, and invited a same-day notice of any violation. DOJ's 7 a.m. report says there is "no prohibition" on sworn officers as monitors, but several were removed (D, ECF 24; row 8).

## What we're watching for
The board tracks several ways federal power could reach this election. They overlap, and none needs the others to happen.
- **Scaring voters away:** armed agents near polls, door-knocks, enforcement surges, threats of prosecution. Tripwires 7, 8, 15.
- **Challenging voters and their ballots:** federal "noncitizen" lists, demands to remove voters or set ballots aside. Tripwires 4, 5.
- **Pressuring the people who count:** criminal threats, grand-jury subpoenas and preservation demands aimed at election officials. Tripwires 2, 6, 14.
- **Targeting candidates and blocking winners:** citizenship and fraud probes of candidates, suits to stop certification, and challenges to seating members when the House convenes Jan 3. Tripwires 9, 10.
- **Investigating organizers:** domestic-terrorism and fraud investigations of registration, union, observer and protest groups (NSPM-7). See "Also watching."
- **The worst case, a declared emergency:** foreign-hack claim → federal "who is a real voter" list → ballots held → cyber incident blamed abroad → FBI takes the "investigation" and the evidence → list used to set aside ballots or block certification before Jan 3. Tripwires 1, 3, 13.
- **What stays after Jan 3:** new data powers and weakened oversight that carry into 2028. Tripwires 11, 12.
