## Phrase families we watch

The trail is how this project finds connections: the same words turning up in an order, then a contract, then a court filing. Every new government document gets checked against it. The ones marked ★ sound like routine paperwork but change who can do what.

- **Who counts as a voter:** "undiluted," "diluting the votes of citizens," "lawful voter"; "likely noncitizen"; "may have subsequently naturalized"; "high confidence"; ★"minor data discrepancies"; "voter eligibility"; ★"verification platforms."
- **Who counts as law enforcement:** ★"law enforcement organization" and "designated law-enforcement entity"; ★"cross-designated"; ★"Special Attorney"; "coordinate with federal immigration authorities"; "investigative or law enforcement function"; ★"as commonly understood"; "administrative or clerical personnel"; ★"law enforcement exception." Full chain: [#1 below](#law-enforcement).
- **What counts as an emergency or attack:** "foreign malign influence"; "significant incident"; ★"public confidence"; "every authorized asset," "whole of government," "no-fail mission"; ★"Executive direction"; "armed enemies"; "election infrastructure."
- **What counts as fraud or crime:** "integrity"; "voter fraud organization"; "organized campaigns"; ★"aiders and abettors"; ★"knowingly"; "criminal aliens."
- **Access to everything:** "single source of truth," "one consolidated record," "person-centric"; "eliminate data silos"; "unfettered access," "any data asset, information, or record"; ★"through subpoena where necessary"; "consistency with Federal requirements"; ★"law enforcement sensitive."
- **Limits written in, then left open:** "solely on the basis"; "voluntary"; "no plans"; ★"will immediately become effective"; "keeps the function under state control"; "for no other purpose." The promises table further down applies this family to what officials have said.

## #1 · "Law enforcement": the label that opens and closes doors {#law-enforcement}

"Law enforcement" carve-outs exist for good reasons: police need records, and investigators need room to work. That's what makes the label useful. Say it out loud and a door opens. Disclaim it and a limit goes away. This year the label has been used three ways:

- **Added:** give a person, office or contract the label, and powers and data follow.
- **Disclaimed:** say "not law enforcement" where the label would trigger limits.
- **Exception:** invoke the label to get past a rule.

## How the trail works

**Tiers**
- **A · Authority:** statute, bill, executive order, memo, directive, OLC opinion, agency plan.
- **B · Procurement and notices:** J&A / LSJ / SSJ / JEFO, SOW or PWS, RFI, solicitation, SORN, PIA, Federal Register rule.
- **C · Reports and filings:** court filings and declarations, press releases, official statements, think-tank or advocacy papers.

**Marks:** D primary text read · R reporting · I inference · U unresolved · K general knowledge, verify.

**What an entry is.** One phrase family. Each row in the chain gives the exact quoted words, document, date, tier and mark. Chains are ordered by document date, not by who wrote what. Order of appearance is a lead, not proof that one document drove another.

**Strength of a match** (rate every entry)
- **S1 exact** · identical wording of 4+ words, or 2–3 distinctive words.
- **S2 near** · same distinctive words, different order or inflection.
- **S3 concept** · same idea, different words (e.g., "golden record" vs "one consolidated record"). Useful for mapping, weakest as provenance.

**Genericity** (rate every entry)
- **G1 boilerplate** · acquisition or legal template language ("logical follow-on," "sole source," "interest of economy and efficiency"). Excluded from the ledger unless it appears in a tier-A text.
- **G2 field-common** · common in data, IT or security writing ("seamless," "unified platform," "single source of truth"). Track, but do not read as signal alone.
- **G3 distinctive** · unusual in that field, or tied to a particular advocacy or legal theory ("undiluted," "diluting the votes of citizens"). Highest value.

**Competing explanations (check for every entry)**
- H1 shared industry vocabulary (MDM, NIEM, vendor marketing).
- H2 common template or the same drafter or counsel office.
- H3 vendor-supplied capability text copied into a government justification (this one matters most for tier B).
- H4 borrowed from an earlier public source (advocacy paper, prior EO, think-tank model).
- H5 deliberate carry-through so the paper trail "fits."
No entry below settles any of these. Vendors stay neutral infrastructure: an overlap in a procurement document is not evidence that a company knew of or took part in anything.

**Log rule.** When a new document arrives, search 2–3 of its distinctive phrases in quotes against this file, the research corpus. Add a row, or add a new entry. Record "no earlier hit found" when that is the result. Quote verbatim from the source text or do not quote.

## What they promised, and what they left room for

Officials have made several promises about federal agents, troops and ballots around the 2026 election. Read the exact words: each one rules out something narrow and leaves the rest open. Armed intimidation at the polls would likely be illegal anyway (18 U.S.C. 592 and 593; Voting Rights Act §11(b)). The point is what the government has gone out of its way not to rule out.

**How to read one.** Ask four questions of any promise: Which agency does it name, and which does it leave out? Which place (polling place, or the drop box, election office and counting center)? Which time (Election Day, or the weeks of counting through Jan 3)? Is it "will not," or only "no plans"?
